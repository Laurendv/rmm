03 — how we help

Three ways we work.
One discipline.

Most clients come to us at one of three moments. The work looks different in each — the standard doesn’t.

Before the dispute escalates

Dispute systems design & conflict prevention

01 / 03

You see the risk forming. Community engagement isn’t holding. The grievance mechanism isn’t credible. Financing covenants are tightening. We map the stakeholders, model the escalation pathways, and design the systems — grievance mechanisms, early resolution frameworks, engagement architectures — that catch disputes before they become public, expensive, or irreversible.

Aligned with the accountability standards of the World Bank Group (CAO/IFC), Asian Development Bank, African Development Bank, European Investment Bank, and Inter-American Development Bank.

Typical engagement 8–16 weeks for design; continuing advisory available.
When the table is fragile

Structured negotiation & process design

02 / 03

Negotiations are happening, but they’re not working. Sequencing is off. The wrong stakeholders are at the table — or the right ones aren’t. Side conversations are undermining the formal process. We design the architecture: who meets, in what order, on what terms. Benefit-sharing frameworks. De-escalation pathways. Settlement structures that won’t collapse under the next political cycle.

This is mandate-based advisory work. We do not act as mediator within the same dispute. Role separation is absolute.

Typical engagement 3–9 months; process- or milestone-based.
When the dispute is live

Independent mediation & neutral facilitation

03 / 03

Parties have agreed they need a neutral. We act only under formal joint appointment of all parties, or within established institutional and IFI accountability frameworks. We design and conduct the mediation, hold procedural integrity, and structure the path from positions to agreement.

CEDR-accredited. Practising in English, Portuguese, Spanish, and French.

Typical mandate From initial joint appointment through agreement and, where requested, implementation review.
Section 04 — A note on roles

Advisory and mediation are never combined within the same dispute or mandate.

Not for convenience. Not for relationship continuity. Not at all.

This isn’t a marketing line — it’s how mediation stays credible. When you appoint RMM as mediator, you can be certain no party at the table has paid for our advisory work on the matter. When you retain RMM for advisory work, you know we will not later present ourselves as a neutral on it.

07 — our approach

Three principles we
don’t compromise on.

  1. 01

    Prevent before resolve.

    Most ESG disputes are designed in long before they erupt. Engagement gaps, weak grievance mechanisms, misaligned incentives — the architecture of the dispute is built years before the headline. The earlier we engage, the more options exist.

  2. 02

    De-escalate with structure.

    Pressure doesn’t ease on its own. It needs a process — sequencing, ground rules, parallel tracks, a clear architecture of who decides what, when. Improvised dialogue rarely survives the first hard week. Structured dialogue does.

  3. 03

    Resolve to hold.

    A settlement that doesn’t survive the next political cycle, the next leadership change, or the next operational milestone isn’t a resolution. We design for durability — built into the agreement, the implementation, and the accountability frame around both.

Founder, RMM Dispute Resolution

Rafaela founded RMM after a decade inside the rooms where the hardest cross-border disputes get decided. At the International Court of Arbitration of the ICC in Paris, she served as Deputy Counsel and managed more than 100 complex commercial and investment arbitrations across energy, infrastructure, and transport — including multi-party and multi-contract disputes involving states and corporations.

She later worked in development finance contexts through a legal and policy fellowship and project-based advisory mandates with international civil society organisations, supporting facilitated dialogue, mediation proceedings, and grievance mechanism processes between companies, communities, governments, and international financial institutions.
