02 — the stakes

When environmental and social risks materialise, they unfold differently across projects.

Transport and logistics

A transport or logistics corridor stalls after stakeholder engagement and public consultation processes break down, leaving unresolved social licence and community acceptance issues that affect long-term implementation.

Mining

A mining project reaches commissioning amid community protest linked to legacy environmental and social impacts that were not adequately addressed during permitting or construction.

Accountability mechanisms

A development finance institution’s accountability mechanism is triggered, initiating a compliance review or dispute resolution process under environmental and social safeguards frameworks, including stakeholder engagement requirements and considerations related to Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC).

The result is consistent: deepening distrust, project delays, and escalating tensions across affected communities, project developers, governments, investors, buyers, and other stakeholders in the value chain.

Once these disputes escalate, they rarely remain confined to questions of law or contract. Community and public pressure, regulatory scrutiny, financing obligations, political dynamics, operational delays, and environmental and social risks begin interacting at the same time.

Most dispute resolution frameworks were built for legal or contractual disagreements. These conflicts are different.

They unfold across institutions, politics, financing relationships, community dynamics, and operational pressures simultaneously, often with cross-boundary and cross-jurisdictional implications that traditional processes are not designed to manage.

Our work is designed for that overlap.

We work with parties to design and lead processes that hold under pressure - across stakeholders, jurisdictions, and governance systems that determine whether agreements hold in practice, including in contexts where unresolved grievances can generate spillover effects beyond the immediate project environment.

03 — how we help

Three ways we work.
One standard of practice.

Most parties come to us at one of three moments. The work differs in each - the approach to conflict prevention, management, and resolution does not.

Before the dispute emerges or escalates

Dispute systems design & conflict prevention

01 / 03

Focus: risk, early signals, system design

A project developer may not yet fully understand where risks are emerging. Community representatives may have concerns but lack a structured process to engage effectively, while intra-community divisions, historical grievances, and uneven distribution of project benefits constrain meaningful participation. Grievance mechanisms are often absent, inaccessible, or not trusted.

We map stakeholders across formal and informal authority structures, assess legal and contextual risk, including local and regional political economy dynamics, and identify early indicators of escalation across community, operational, and governance levels. We model how small grievances can compound into broader legitimacy challenges or conflicts if left unaddressed.

We design engagement and dispute systems together with parties before escalation occurs, from credible grievance mechanisms and early resolution pathways to FPIC-informed consultation architectures and structured dialogue processes that allow issues to surface safely, be verified, and be addressed at the lowest possible level.

This reduces uncertainty in high-risk environments where large-scale infrastructure, extractive activity, or energy transition projects intersect with complex governance contexts, historical dynamics, and varying levels of institutional trust.

Standards alignment Conducted in alignment with development finance institutions’ social and environmental standards and safeguards frameworks.
When tensions are emerging or negotiations are stalled

Structured negotiation & process design

02 / 03

Focus: misalignment, sequencing, negotiation structure and strategy, implementation gaps, negotiated agreement(s)

There is a willingness to negotiate, or negotiations are underway but not yet producing a durable outcome. Core interests may not be fully structured or sequenced, benefit expectations may be misaligned, and parties may be engaging through formal and informal channels that slow convergence.

We support the party that has retained us in structuring and advancing its negotiation position in complex, multi-stakeholder environments. This includes clarifying interests, constraints, as well as trade-offs, sequencing issues to enable progress, and shaping negotiation strategy.

We support negotiation processes through stakeholder mapping, position development, sequencing of engagement, and issue framing to enable clearer and more durable outcomes.

This includes distributive and implementation issues such as benefit-sharing, compensation structures, and operational commitments, particularly where negotiations are affected by parallel processes, shifting political conditions, or fragmented engagement channels.

Mandate type Mandate-based negotiation advisory work.
When the dispute is live

Independent mediation & neutral facilitation

03 / 03

Focus: dispute resolution, communication breakdown, mediated agreement(s)

When a dispute is active, particularly in complex company-community or multi-stakeholder contexts, parties may require a structured, neutral process to support engagement, reduce escalation, and create conditions for negotiated resolution.

Rafaela Menezes de Magalhães acts as a mediator or facilitator in complex, multi-stakeholder disputes under formal joint appointment of all parties, whether on an ad hoc basis or within established institutional or development finance accountability frameworks.

Her role is to support the parties in reaching their own resolution by facilitating communication, clarifying issues, positions, and interests, and assisting in the structured exploration of options for agreement in environments where negotiations may be fragmented, asymmetrical, or affected by differing institutional, social, or operational constraints.

Drawing on her professional experience and multicultural background, she is able to navigate between different institutional and cultural perspectives, supporting mutual understanding and constructive engagement.

All mediation processes are conducted in accordance with established principles of mediation practice, including neutrality, confidentiality, party autonomy, and a structured, process-based framework designed to support the parties in moving from positions toward a negotiated outcome.

Credentials and role separation CEDR-accredited. Practising in English, Portuguese, Spanish, and French. Rafaela Menezes de Magalhães does not act as a mediator or neutral facilitator in any dispute in which she has previously provided negotiation advisory support.

07 — about the founder

Rafaela Menezes de Magalhães

Founder, RMM Dispute Resolution

Rafaela founded RMM Dispute Resolution after a decade working both inside the rooms where complex cross-border commercial and investment disputes are decided and on the ground with affected communities where those same projects are contested through social and environmental concerns, political dynamics, public scrutiny, and evolving accountability frameworks.

At the International Court of Arbitration of the International Chamber of Commerce, she served as Deputy Counsel and managed more than 100 commercial and investment arbitrations across the energy, infrastructure, and transport sectors, including multi-party and multi-contract disputes involving states, state-owned entities, and multinational corporations.

She later worked in development finance contexts through a legal and policy fellowship and project-based advisory mandates with international civil society organisations, supporting dialogue processes, mediation proceedings, and grievance mechanism frameworks involving companies, communities, governments, and international financial institutions, including the World Bank Group (IFC/CAO).

Her experience across international arbitration and field-based engagement in politically and socially complex project environments has shaped a practice focused on how disputes form, escalate, and can be structurally addressed across legal, operational, and legitimacy dimensions before, during, and beyond formal proceedings.
